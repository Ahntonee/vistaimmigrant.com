Removal Proceedings

Representation in immigration court when the government seeks removal.

Removal proceedings begin when the government gives you a Notice to Appear (NTA) and files it with an immigration court. The notice lists the reasons the government says you can be removed from the United States and tells you where and when to appear.

It is very important to attend every hearing and keep your address up to date with the court. If you miss a hearing, the judge can order you removed in your absence. You have the right to be represented by an attorney in immigration court, though not at government expense. Our licensed attorney can represent you in matters she accepts.

Who this is for

  • Anyone who received a Notice to Appear
  • Families supporting a loved one in immigration court
  • People unsure whether they have a hearing scheduled

What to expect

The attorney reviews the NTA and your history, explains the charges and whether any forms of relief might apply, and prepares you for each hearing. Vista staff can help you keep track of dates and organize paperwork.

What to bring

  • The Notice to Appear and every court notice you have received
  • Passport, ID, I-94 and any immigration papers
  • A list of every address where you have lived
  • Any court or criminal records related to your case
This service is provided by our licensed attorney for matters the attorney accepts. Vista staff who are not attorneys do not provide legal advice. This page is general information and does not replace advice about your own situation.

Questions

I got a Notice to Appear. What should I do first?

Do not ignore it. Write down the hearing date, keep the paper safe, and speak with an attorney quickly. You can also check your case status through the immigration court’s online system.

What if I missed a hearing?

Contact an attorney right away. In some situations an order made in your absence can be challenged, but strict deadlines apply.
