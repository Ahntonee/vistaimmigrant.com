Motions to Reopen

Asking the court to reopen a case after a decision has been made.

A motion to reopen asks the immigration court or the Board of Immigration Appeals to look at a case again after a final decision. It usually must be based on new facts or evidence. In general there are time and number limits, with limited exceptions, for example in some cases where a person did not receive notice of a hearing or where conditions in the home country have changed.

Filing a motion does not always stop a removal order from being carried out. Because the deadlines are strict, speak to an attorney as soon as you can. Our attorney can represent you in matters she accepts.

Who this is for

  • People who have a removal order and believe they have new evidence or circumstances
  • People who say they never received notice of a hearing
  • Families trying to understand whether any option remains

What to expect

The attorney reviews your entire file, tells you honestly whether a motion is realistic, and if she accepts the matter, prepares the motion, evidence and any request to pause removal.

What to bring

  • Every decision, order and notice you received
  • The documents that show what is new or what changed
  • Proof of your addresses over time
This service is provided by our licensed attorney for matters the attorney accepts. Vista staff who are not attorneys do not provide legal advice. This page is general information and does not replace advice about your own situation.

Questions

Is there a deadline to file?

Yes. There are time limits, and they are different depending on your situation. Do not wait.
