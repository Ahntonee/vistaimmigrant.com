Green Card / Adjustment of Status

Applying for lawful permanent residence while you are in the United States.

Adjustment of status is the process of applying for a green card (lawful permanent residence) without leaving the United States. It is filed on Form I-485. Whether you qualify depends on the basis for your application, such as a family relationship, employment, or a grant of asylum, and on factors like how you entered the country, your history, and whether a visa number is available.

Because mistakes can affect more than one application, the choice of strategy matters. Our licensed attorney can review your situation and represent you in matters she accepts.

Who this is for

  • Spouses, parents and children of U.S. citizens
  • Family members of permanent residents with an approved petition
  • People with approved asylum or another qualifying basis

What to expect

The attorney reviews eligibility, then prepares the application and evidence. You will usually attend a biometrics appointment and may be called to an interview. A medical examination by an authorized doctor is generally part of the process.

What to bring

  • Passport, birth certificate (with English translation if needed)
  • I-94, visas and any prior immigration filings or notices
  • Marriage certificate and proof of relationship, if family-based
  • Tax returns, proof of income, and police records if you have them
This service is provided by our licensed attorney for matters the attorney accepts. Vista staff who are not attorneys do not provide legal advice. This page is general information and does not replace advice about your own situation.

Questions

How long does a green card application take?

It varies widely by category, location and workload. We will explain what to expect for your case, but we cannot promise a date.

Do I need to leave the country to apply?

Many people can apply from inside the United States, but not everyone. Eligibility is individual, so please get advice before traveling or filing.
